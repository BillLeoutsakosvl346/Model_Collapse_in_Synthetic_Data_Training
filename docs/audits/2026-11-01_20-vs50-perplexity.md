# Audit: why does 20% synthetic continued training have higher final perplexity than 50%?

Four fresh agents independently audited mixture construction, actual deployed training/settings, evaluation/comparison arithmetic and primary literature. This was read-only inspection of code and saved evidence, plus web research. No Runpod API calls, SSH sessions, model execution, training, cloud changes or changes to published scientific results were performed.

## Finding

No concrete implementation, source-selection, checkpoint-policy or reporting error was found that explains the reversal. The saved trajectories genuinely differ. This is not proof of universally bug-free execution: bulk production weights/full training arrays were not independently restored or re-evaluated during this audit.

Final mean test NLL differs by 0.13904380695 nats/target. Exponentiating yields a 14.9174% PPL difference. The 20% model is worse on 3,757/4,494 test blocks (83.60%); the 10%-trimmed mean gap is still 0.136902 nats, and every decile of original-S0 block difficulty has a positive average gap. Thus a few unusually difficult passages do not explain the result. These are descriptive observations within one seeded pair, not independent training replicates or an across-seed significance test.

## What was checked

- Full saved configs differ only in experiment ID and synthetic fraction. Recorded GPU/software environments match. Per-stage configs agree with their parent configurations and recorded accepted runtimes.
- Exact pinned S0, original D1 pool and all real split hashes match. The real blocks are selected from training only; no validation/test source is used.
- All ten 50% mixtures contain 18,973 real and 18,973 synthetic blocks. All ten 20% mixtures contain 30,357 real and 7,589 synthetic blocks. Every mixture has 37,946 blocks of 64 tokens.
- All twenty actual saved source selections, permutations and source-label arrays match independent deterministic reconstruction. Sampling is without replacement within a source/cycle. Real subsets are nested: the 50% real selection is contained in the 80% real selection in every cycle. Synthetic index positions are nested too; later synthetic contents differ because the models differ.
- Initializer, generator, synthetic-pool and actual mixed-input hashes form coherent recorded handoffs. Deployed training/data/selection source files match across the two accepted runtime bundles.
- Both arms execute 14,850 optimizer updates, five epochs per cycle, identical learning-rate schedules and fresh AdamW/scheduler state each cycle. Final partial batches use correct microbatch weighting. All stages select epoch 5 and record matching terminal/selected tensor hashes. Event logs contain fifty epoch completions per arm without recorded training-reuse/restart events.
- All 22 test metric rows were independently recalculated from the same 4,494 blocks and 283,122 shifted targets. Published curves use corpus PPL, not arithmetic mean passage PPL. Ratio labels and checkpoint/run identities are correct.

## Interpretation: plausible mechanisms, not an identified cause

**Repeated real-data fitting is the leading hypothesis.** Increasing the real fraction does not add new independent real text: it increases the frequency of updates on the same finite WikiText training pool. Over ten cycles and five epochs, real blocks receive about 40 additional exposures on average at 20% synthetic, versus 25 at 50%. Stronger fitting of those particular articles can reduce generalization to unseen articles. Prior language-model research demonstrates that repeated-data training can cause multi-epoch degradation, but does not establish that it caused this pair's reversal.

**Recursive feedback also changes the experiment over time.** Only S0/D1 are common across ratios. Different S1 checkpoints generate different D2 pools, and that difference propagates. This is not a comparison of the same fixed synthetic texts at different weights. Changing the ratio affects both the optimization trajectory and later target content. More synthetic targets might partly constrain changes through self-distillation, but this is a hypothesis, not a measured regularization effect in these runs.

**Retaining epoch 5 alone does not explain the ordering.** At cycle 10, validation NLL after epoch 1 is already 4.157633 at 20% versus 4.018098 at 50%. Epochs 2–5 worsen it by 0.107161 and 0.111668, respectively. Both are harmed by additional within-cycle fitting, and the extra harm is slightly smaller at 20%. The evidence therefore does not show greater overfitting *within the final stage* at 20%; any overfitting explanation must account for the cumulative trajectory.

The recorded training loss is an online, dropout-enabled mean over mixed-source targets while weights change. It is not the final checkpoint's loss on real training text separately. It cannot by itself demonstrate real-data memorization. Seed dependence and nonlinear generated-data effects remain unresolved; this is not evidence that 50% is universally optimal or real data is intrinsically harmful.

## Relation to the papers

[Seddik et al., sections 2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05090v1) study a statistical conditional-probability estimator and a character-model simulation with a different reference distribution/metric. Their bounds do not guarantee a monotonic ordering of held-out human WikiText perplexity under our carried-weight, final-epoch-5 OPT training. Their partial-synthetic theory also uses fixed original empirical data; it would be inaccurate to explain this discrepancy simply by claiming that they always use fresh human data.

[Xue et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/b9e472cd579c83e2f6aa3459f46aac28-Abstract-Conference.html) empirically document multi-epoch degradation from repeated training data. [Muennighoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264) show that repetition can still help before diminishing returns, so repetition is not automatically harmful. [Shumailov et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11269175/) distinguish statistical, expressivity and optimization errors; their OPT experiment's initialization/checkpoint/seed protocol differs from this continued-weight comparison.

## Most useful follow-up checks (not executed)

1. Score existing checkpoints on fixed real-training, real-validation and synthetic-target blocks separately, with dropout disabled. Compare real train/held-out gaps by ratio and cycle. This would test the cumulative overfitting hypothesis without new training.
2. Analyse the already-running matched 20% reset arm when its sealed results are available. Removing carried parameter history is informative but does not by itself isolate the mechanism.
3. Later, with separate authorization, repeat multiple seeds and add a matched real-only continued control. A same-initializer/common-synthetic-pool single-cycle experiment would isolate immediate mixture effects from recursive feedback.
